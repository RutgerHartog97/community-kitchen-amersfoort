Olive-oil cake

Baking · Cake

A moist olive-oil cake with orange zest and rosemary.

Equipment

  • Springform or cake tin (Ø 24–26 cm)
  • 2 mixing bowls
  • Sieve
  • Whisk or hand mixer
  • Spatula
  • Grater (for the zest)
  • Measuring cups & scale
  • Baking paper

Ingredients

  • 280 g flour (sifted)
  • 150 g caster sugar
  • 1½ tsp baking powder
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • 3 large eggs (room temp.)
  • 240 ml extra-virgin olive oil
  • 180 ml (Greek) yoghurt
  • 1–2 tbsp fresh rosemary, chopped
  • Zest of 1 organic (blood) orange
  • Optional: coarse sugar for the top
  • Glaze (optional): juice of 1 orange
  • Glaze (optional): 120 g icing sugar
  • Glaze (optional): zest of 1 orange

Method

  1. Preheat the oven to 180 °C and grease the tin (line the base with baking paper if you like).
  2. Mix the dry ingredients in a bowl.
  3. In another bowl, whisk the eggs with the oil and yoghurt, then stir in the orange zest and rosemary.
  4. Fold the wet mix through the dry into a smooth batter and pour into the tin. Sprinkle coarse sugar on top if using.
  5. Bake until a skewer comes out clean. Cool briefly in the tin, then fully on a rack.
  6. No coarse sugar? Make a glaze by mixing the orange juice with icing sugar and zest, and pour over the cake.

Labneh

Side · Dip

Thick strained yoghurt, finished with good olive oil and za'atar.

Equipment

  • 2 bowls
  • Sieve or colander (fits the bowl)
  • Tea towel or cheesecloth

Ingredients

  • 500 g full-fat (Greek) yoghurt
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Mix the yoghurt with the salt in a bowl.
  2. Line a sieve/colander with the cloth over the second bowl, spoon in the yoghurt and fold the cloth closed.
  3. Chill and drain for a few hours or overnight, longer draining means firmer labneh.
  4. Serve with a generous drizzle of good olive oil and, if you like, za'atar and/or sumac.

Quick-pickled red onion

Condiment

Bright quick pickles that keep for weeks.

Equipment

  • Mandoline or sharp knife
  • Cutting board
  • Pan
  • Glass preserving jar

Ingredients

  • 3 red onions
  • 120 ml natural vinegar
  • 120 ml water
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp cane sugar (or agave/honey)
  • 1 tbsp whole spices (pepper, fennel, cumin, coriander, mustard…)
  • 2 bay leaves

Method

  1. Slice the onion as thinly as possible with a mandoline or sharp knife.
  2. Put the water, vinegar and all spices in a pan and bring to the boil.
  3. Add the onion and cook, stirring, for 30–60 seconds.
  4. Turn off the heat and let it cool completely.
  5. Transfer to a clean jar; keeps up to 3 weeks in the fridge. Check it looks, smells and tastes good after that.

Source: Based on Feasting At Home ↗

Parsnip risotto

Main · Risotto

Creamy parsnip risotto with flower sprouts, walnuts and leek oil.

Ingredients

  • Parsnip
  • Thyme
  • Rosemary
  • Salt
  • Onion
  • Garlic
  • Risotto rice
  • White wine
  • Stock of choice
  • Parmesan
  • Flower sprouts
  • Olive oil
  • Lemon juice
  • Walnuts
  • Leek tops + neutral oil (for the oil)

Method

  1. Parsnip sauce, cut the parsnip into 2–3 cm cubes and grill until browned; add thyme.
  2. Cover with water, cook until a fork slides through easily, then blend to a purée. Season with rosemary and salt; taste and adjust.
  3. Risotto, sweat chopped onion and garlic until glassy, add the rice and toast until glassy too.
  4. Deglaze with white wine and cook until reduced by half.
  5. Add stock a ladle at a time, stirring, until creamy and just holding its shape (keep 2 ladles back). Stir the parsnip sauce in with a ladle of stock.
  6. Off the heat (low), stir through Parmesan.
  7. Flower sprouts, marinate with olive oil and lemon juice; roast at 180 °C until crisp.
  8. Walnuts, roast with salt until dark brown.
  9. Leek oil, roast leftover leek tops until lightly charred, blend with a neutral oil and strain.

Tarragon mayonnaise

Condiment · Mayo

Ingredients

  • 4–6 eggs or egg yolks
  • 700 ml sunflower oil
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
  • 15 sprigs tarragon
  • 1 pinch salt
  • 1–2 tsp sugar

Method

  1. Make sure everything is at room temperature so the emulsion doesn't split.
  2. Put the eggs, lemon juice, mustard, salt and sugar in a food processor and pulse to combine.
  3. With the machine running, pour the oil in a thin, steady stream through the feed tube, too fast at the start will split it.
  4. Keep pouring until all the oil is absorbed and it's thick, pale and creamy (about 2–3 min).

Chard tahini dip

Side · Dip

Ingredients

  • 700 g chard
  • 150 ml olive oil
  • 5 garlic cloves
  • 120 ml tahini
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Remove the ribs and stems from the leaves and chop finely; tear the leaves into small pieces. Keep both separate.
  2. Heat 1/3 cup oil in a large pan over medium-low. Cook the ribs and stems (covered), stirring and adding a splash of water if browning, until soft (5–7 min). Add garlic; cook until fragrant (~1 min).
  3. Add the leaves a handful at a time, letting them wilt before adding more, until all soft (10–12 min). Cool, then squeeze out excess moisture (about 1/2 cup).
  4. Blend the chard with 1 tbsp cooking liquid, the tahini, lemon juice and 1/3 cup oil. Season with salt and blend creamy with only small bits visible, adding cooking liquid if needed (up to 5 min).

Chive oil

Condiment · Oil

Ingredients

  • 125 g chives
  • 1 L vegetable oil

Method

  1. Wash the chives and pat dry to remove excess moisture.
  2. Blend the chives with the oil until smooth.
  3. Rest 2–3 days in the fridge in a glass or non-reactive container.
  4. Strain through a fine sieve (e.g. a chinois) to keep only the coloured oil.
  5. Keep up to 1 month in an airtight container.

Lettuce soup

Soup

Ingredients

  • 20 garlic cloves
  • 1 block butter
  • Chive oil (to finish)
  • (White-wine) vinegar
  • Vegetable stock (ideally from leftover veg)
  • Courgette, peels blanched + ice bath for colour
  • 3 large potatoes (to bind)
  • Parsley
  • Fresh mint
  • Parsnip (fried, on top)
  • Zest + juice of 5–6 lemons
  • Chilli pepper
  • 800 ml–1 L cream

Method

  1. Prep, peel the courgette; blanch the peels and shock in ice water. Slice the courgette.
  2. Chop onion & garlic, dice the potato small, chop parsley, slice chilli and spiralize the parsnip.
  3. Topping, deep-fry the parsnip spirals in sunflower oil.
  4. Sweat onion and garlic in butter; add courgette and cook until lightly caramelised; season with salt.
  5. Deglaze with a little vinegar and add the diced potato.
  6. Add half the stock and cook until the potato is just done. Add the lettuce, mix well and blend with a hand blender.
  7. Add the rest of the stock to the desired thickness (a touch thick, the cream thins it). Stir in lemon zest & juice, fresh chilli, mint & parsley.
  8. Add cream to the desired texture and taste; adjust with salt or lemon. Finish with the parsnip crisps & chive oil.

Leek three ways

Starter · Leek

Ingredients

  • 1 leek
  • 1 part cooking cream
  • 1 part stock
  • 1 shallot
  • 1 lemon
  • Olive oil
  • 1 garlic clove

Method

  1. Leek sauce, cut the leek into pieces (green leaves and middle; skip the root end).
  2. Char the green leaves on a hot barbecue until fairly blackened.
  3. Chop the shallot and sweat until lightly coloured.
  4. Add cream, stock and charred leek and cook for 15 minutes.
  5. Off the heat, add lemon juice, strain and cool; season with salt. Transfer to a cream whipper and charge.
  6. Fried leek, cut into fine julienne, deep-fry until golden and drain on kitchen paper.
  7. Sous-vide leek, chop the garlic small; bag the leek, garlic and a dash of olive oil and vacuum-seal.
  8. Cook sous-vide 30 min at 85 °C, then grill briefly in a pan or with a blowtorch.
  9. Pipe the sauce onto the plate, lay the leek on the sauce and the fried leek on top.

Pumpkin-miso pasta sauce

Main · Sauce

Ingredients

  • ½ pumpkin
  • 1 tbsp miso paste
  • 100 g butter
  • 1 vegetable/mushroom stock (umami)
  • 5 sage leaves
  • 1 tin coconut milk
  • Parmesan (to taste)
  • 1–2 tbsp ricotta
  • Pinch of cumin
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Cut the pumpkin into half-moons or large pieces (it gets puréed anyway).
  2. Blanch 2–3 minutes, then dry on kitchen paper.
  3. Char the outside with a blowtorch until lightly blackened.
  4. Blend the pumpkin with 10 g butter to a mass.
  5. Add the miso, cumin and stock and blend to a smooth sauce (a little water if too thick, carefully).
  6. Pour into the pan, add the coconut milk and reduce. Before serving, stir in a tablespoon of ricotta.
  7. Brown sage butter, cut the butter into large pieces in a light-coloured pan over heat.
  8. Once it foams, add the dry sage and cook 3–5 minutes without touching or stirring.
  9. It foams and browns; take off the heat when the foam subsides, the sage should be crisp.
  10. Toss the sauce through fresh pasta or gnocchi, grate over Parmesan and spoon 2–3 tbsp sage butter over the plate.

Baked pears

Dessert

Caramelised baked pears with a cracked kletskop tuile, lemon-verbena cream and sorbet.

Ingredients

  • Pears, 15–20 cooking pears, ½ pp (Comice/Conference, ripe but firm)
  • Pears, butter, brown caster sugar, cinnamon, nutmeg
  • Pears, juice of 3 lemons
  • Kletskop, 60 g almonds, finely chopped
  • Kletskop, 125 g flour, 275 g caster sugar, 3.5 g cinnamon
  • Kletskop, 75 g butter (room temp.), 50 ml water
  • Verbena cream, 1 L cream (35%), handful lemon verbena, 1–2 tbsp sugar
  • Finish, yoghurt sorbet (Vitelli) or lemon, 1 scoop pp

Method

  1. Lemon-verbena cream (start ahead). Option A, crème fraîche (12 h): vacuum-bag cream, buttermilk, sugar and bruised verbena; sous-vide 8–12 h at 35 °C; strain and set 4 h in the fridge. Option B, infused cream (2 h): vacuum-bag cream, sugar and verbena; sous-vide 2 h at 55 °C; strain, chill and whip later.
  2. Kletskop tuile, mix butter, sugar, flour, cinnamon and water to a smooth dough; fold in the almonds. Spread one thin layer on a lined tray and bake at 175 °C for 10–12 min until golden and bubbling. Cool until glass-hard, then crack into shards.
  3. Baked pears, preheat to 200 °C. Peel, halve lengthwise and core the pears. Place cut-side down; dot with butter and brown sugar, dust with cinnamon and nutmeg, sprinkle with lemon juice. Bake 20–25 min until soft and caramelised, spooning caramel over halfway.
  4. Plate up, lay a warm pear half at an angle with some warm caramel. Add a scoop of sorbet (the acidity cuts the sweetness), a dollop of chilled verbena cream, a tall kletskop shard for height and crumbles for crunch.

Source: Inspired by Let the Baking Begin & Bakkriebels ↗
